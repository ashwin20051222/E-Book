Book IV · On the Retreat Within 142

§ 3. The Unshakable Citadel

Men look for retreats for themselves, the country cottages, shores, and mountains. You too are especially wont to yearn for such things. But this is the work of an ordinary nature, since you may at any hour you please retreat into yourself.

For nowhere does a man retire with more quietness or freedom than into his own soul;

especially if he has within him thoughts of such a nature that by looking into them he immediately finds himself in complete contentment: and by contentment I mean nothing other than well-ordered composure.

Constantly give yourself this retreat, and renew yourself. Let your principles be brief and fundamental, which as soon as you have recurred to them will be sufficient to cleanse the soul and send you back without any irritation to the life whereto you return.

Remember that all things are mere opinion, and opinion rests with yourself. Eliminate it whenever you will, and then, as if you had doubled a cape, there will be calm, quiet water, and a sheltered bay without a wave.

Marginal Note · Added Yesterday

"The inner citadel metaphor applied to civic duty: the quiet center is not an escape from society, but preparation to endure it with generosity."

Whatever may happen to you, it was prepared for you from all eternity; and the implication of causes was from eternity spinning the thread of your being, and of that which is incident to it.

Whether the universe is a concourse of atoms, or Nature is a system, let this first be established: that I am a part of the whole which is governed by Nature.
