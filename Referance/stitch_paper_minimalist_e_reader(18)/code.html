Meditations · Book IV

On Inner Citadel & Composure

People look for retreats for themselves, in the country, by the coast, or in the hills. There is nowhere that a person can find a more peaceful and trouble-free retreat than in his own mind, especially if he has within himself the kind of thoughts which an immediate contemplation will ease the spirit entirely.

By ease I mean nothing other than harmonious order. Grant yourself this retreat continually, and renew yourself. Let your basic principles be brief and fundamental, the kind which will at once, on first encounter, cleanse your soul and send you back without any irritation to the life to which you must return.

“Remember that your directing mind becomes unvanquishable when it withdraws into itself and rests content, doing nothing contrary to its deliberate intent.”

For what is it that irritates you? The badness of men? Recall the conclusion that rational beings exist for the sake of one another; that tolerance is part of justice; that people do wrong involuntarily.

Think of all those who spent their lives in violent hatred, suspicion, hostility, and are now dead, reduced to ashes. Be calm, then.
